## Facts (sources)
- 20 Fenchurch Street ("Walkie-Talkie"), City of London. Wider at the top; its curved (concave) south face focused sunlight onto the street in late summer 2013. Nicknamed "Walkie-Scorchie".
- Martin Lindsay parked his Jaguar XJ on Eastcheap for an hour or two; the wing mirror, Jaguar badge and panels melted/warped.
- Other businesses reported heat damage; a reporter fried an egg on the pavement in the beam.
- The developers paid about £1,000 for the repairs, including the wing mirror.
- A temporary sunshade went up in early 2014 and a permanent one later in 2014.
- Sources: Channel 4 News (3 Sep 2013), UPI (3 Sep 2013), ArchDaily, Wikipedia "20 Fenchurch Street".
- Verified 2026-11-16: roof height 160 m (Skyscraper Center, Wikipedia); cost over £200 m (Wikipedia, Urban75); Sky Garden roof is ~4,800 m² of laminated glass (Construction Management).

## Script (~205 words)
A London skyscraper melted a car from across the street.
It's 2013, and the City's newest tower has a strange shape: it gets fatter as it goes up, and its whole front curves inward.
One sunny afternoon, a man parks his shiny Jaguar on the street right below it.
He's back in an hour.
The wing mirror is drooping.
The badge has gone soft.
The side panels have warped like candle wax.
Up and down the street, shops start complaining about scorch marks.
And one reporter walks out with an egg, cracks it onto the pavement, and fries it.
Luckily, the developers paid for the repairs.
Unluckily for them, the building had cost two hundred million pounds, and the car bill was about one thousand.
You see, a flat window bounces sunlight straight back.
But look into a shiny spoon: the curve gathers all that light into one spot.
Now make that spoon a hundred and sixty metres tall, out of glass, and point it at a parking space.
So the owners bolted a giant sunshade of metal fins across the front, and the street cooled down.
And what did they put at the very top of the tower?
A garden. Under a glass roof.
